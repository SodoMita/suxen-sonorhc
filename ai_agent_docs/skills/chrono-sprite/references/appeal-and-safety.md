# Appeal and safety

Appeal is silhouette, a memory point someone can name, and an expression that is not blank. Those three survive a thumbnail, a sticker, and a store page. They also work for every character in this cast.

Exposure is not a lever here. The blueprint's costume-accident lines are not production direction. `LEGAL.md` is.

## Tracks

The source JSON sets `appeal_track`. It is a limit, not a costume brief.

| Track | Who | What carries the appeal |
|---|---|---|
| `shadow` | Ren, minor-coded | The absent face, the eyes, the uniform as a uniform |
| `cool` | Kira, Nova, Aria, ages not locked | Ears and tail, the hood and the pane, wings and the ring. Competence and a readable outline |
| `presence` | Aurora, Elara, Selene | The pane, the crescents, the staff. Calm, cloth, and a prop that means something |

Working clothes that fit the job read as more specific than a generic costume, not less. Kira's scout jacket, Nova's too-big coat, Ren's gakuran, Elara's vestments.

## Hard limits

Copied into every prompt's exclusion section by the assembler, and repeated here so a hand-written prompt does not drop them:

- Fully clothed. No transparent clothing. No sexual pose. No child proportions.
- No living-artist name.
- No readable text, watermark, or signature.
- A character's own `banned` list, verbatim.

Scars or marks that a future approved doc assigns to a character are drawn as fact, not as spectacle. None are assigned today. Do not invent one.

## What not to do with a shipped file

Kira's shipped portrait, Aurora's shipped suit, and Nova's fitted open jacket are off the production spec. Matching them is not "staying on model". The level doc says so. Follow the level doc.
